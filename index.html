What are the components of a salary calculator?

Calculating your take-home pay involves subtracting all statutory deductions from your total earnings. Use this standard formula to verify your payslip.

Net Salary = Gross Salary – Total Deductions
Standard Malaysian payroll calculation logic

Total Deductions typically include contributions to EPF, SOCSO, EIS, and PCB (Monthly Tax Deduction).

Salary Terms Comparison

Quick reference guide to the most common statutory terms found on Malaysian payslips.

Term Description Impact on Net Pay
EPF (KWSP) Employee Provident Fund for retirement savings. Reduces current net pay; increases long-term savings.
SOCSO (PERKESO) Social security protection for workplace injuries. Small deduction for comprehensive insurance coverage.
EIS (SIP) Employment Insurance System for job loss protection. Minimal deduction to provide a safety net during unemployment.
PCB (MTD) Monthly Tax Deduction for income tax payments. Varies based on your specific annual income bracket.

Salary Inclusions & Exclusions

A precise breakdown of which salary components are subject to statutory deductions.

1. EPF (KWSP)

✓ Included Items
  • Basic salary
  • Bonuses and incentives
  • Commissions
  • Allowances (unless exempted)
  • Arrears of wages
✕ Excluded Items
  • Service charges
  • Overtime payments
  • Gratuity and retirement benefits
  • Travel allowances

Both SOCSO and EIS follow the same definition of "wages" for contribution purposes.

  • Basic salary
  • Overtime payments

  • Paid leave payments
  • Fixed or variable allowances
✕ Excluded Items
  • EPF contributions
  • Traveling allowances
  • Gratuity and retirement benefits
  • Annual bonus

How Your Salary is Calculated – RM5,000 Example

Want to understand exactly what gets deducted from your payslip? Here is a real example using a gross monthly salary of RM5,000, calculated using 2026 official government rates. This example applies to a single Malaysian citizen under 55 years old.

What Gets Deducted From You (Employee)

Item Calculation Amount
Gross Monthly Salary — RM 5,000.00
EPF 11% of RM5,000 - RM 550.00
SOCSO Official PERKESO table - RM 7.50
EIS Official PERKESO table - RM 10.00
PCB (Tax) Progressive rate - RM 97.00
Take Home Pay RM 4,335.50

What Your Employer Pays On Top

Item Calculation Amount
EPF Employer 13% of RM5,000 + RM 650.00
SOCSO Employer Official PERKESO table + RM 70.25
EIS Employer Same as employee rate + RM 10.00
Total Cost to Employer RM 5,730.25

Your employer pays an extra RM730.25 on top of your salary every month — this never appears on your payslip but is the real cost of hiring you.

How PCB RM97.00 Was Worked Out

  1. Monthly salary after EPF: RM5,000 − RM550 = RM4,450
  2. Annualise it: RM4,450 × 12 = RM53,400
  3. Deduct individual relief: RM53,400 − RM9,000 = RM44,400 taxable income
  4. Tax on first RM5,000: 0% = RM0
  5. Tax on next RM15,000: 1% = RM150
  6. Tax on next RM15,000: 3% = RM450
  7. Tax on remaining RM9,400: 6% = RM564
  8. Total annual tax: RM1,164 ÷ 12 = RM97.00/month

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate are these calculations?

All calculations strictly adhere to official schedules published by statutory government bodies:

  • Statutory Accuracy: Follows exact contribution tables and formulas from KWSP (EPF), PERKESO (SOCSO & EIS), and LHDN (PCB income tax).
  • Latest Rates: Updated for the current tax year including wage ceilings and tax relief brackets.
Note: Results are estimates. Actual payslip amounts may vary slightly due to company-specific allowance taxability, individual relief claims, or special exemptions.
Is my salary data saved anywhere?

No. Your privacy is fully guaranteed during every session:

  • Client-Side Processing: Every calculation runs 100% locally inside your browser.
  • Zero Data Stored: We maintain 0 data stored — no tracking, database logging, or external analytics on your financial inputs.
  • Complete Confidentiality: Your salary details never leave your device.
What should I enter as gross salary?

Gross salary represents total pre-deduction earnings:

  • Base Monthly Pay: Enter your basic salary before any statutory deductions.
  • Fixed Allowances: Include recurring fixed monthly allowances (e.g., transport, housing, position allowances).
  • Variable Earnings: Exclude one-off bonuses or irregular overtime; use the dedicated Bonus / Allowance input fields for variable pay.
Why is my PCB showing RM0?

Monthly Tax Deduction (PCB) only applies once chargeable income exceeds mandatory income thresholds:

  • Single Individuals: Income after EPF must exceed RM 2,851 / month.
  • Married Individuals: Threshold rises to RM 3,851 / month (with non-working spouse).
  • Tax Relief Reductions: Personal, medical, and child tax reliefs further lower your monthly PCB deduction.
Important: Even if your monthly PCB deduction is RM 0, you may still need to file an annual income tax return with LHDN via MyTax if your total annual income exceeds RM 34,000.
Why does SOCSO stop at RM6,000?

Statutory contributions for social security have a fixed maximum threshold:

  • Statutory Wage Ceiling: Both PERKESO (SOCSO) and EIS contributions are capped at the RM 6,000 wage limit.
  • Capped Rate: Earnings above RM 6,000 / month pay the exact same fixed maximum contribution as an RM 6,000 salary.
  • Updated Regulation: The ceiling was enforced by PERKESO in October 2024 (up from RM 5,000).
Does this calculator work for foreigners?

Yes. Select Foreigner / Non-Malaysian under Nationality settings:

  • Foreigner EPF Rate: Foreign employees are subject to mandatory EPF contributions at 2% employee + 2% employer.
  • Non-Resident PCB Tax: Charged at a flat 30% rate unless tax residency (182+ days in Malaysia) is established with LHDN.
  • Exemptions: EIS contributions do not apply to foreign employees.
How often are the rates updated?

We continuously maintain alignment with official government policies:

  • Annual Tax Sync: Updated every January to incorporate new Budget tax reliefs and LHDN formulas.
  • Immediate Gazette Sync: Mid-year statutory updates (such as the RM 6,000 SOCSO ceiling or EPF rate updates) are applied immediately upon enforcement by KWSP and PERKESO.
What is the difference between employee and employer contributions?

Statutory contributions are split between payroll deductions and employer costs:

  • Employee Deductions: Subtracted directly from gross salary, reducing your net take-home pay.
  • Employer Contributions: Paid separately by your company directly to KWSP, PERKESO, and EIS without reducing your gross pay.
  • Total Cost of Employment (TCOE): Represents the complete financial expenditure required by an employer (Gross Salary + Employer Statutory Contributions).
