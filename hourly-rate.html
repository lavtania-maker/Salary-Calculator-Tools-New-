What is an Hourly Rate?

An hourly rate is the fixed amount of money earned for every single hour of work performed. Unlike a fixed monthly salary, hourly compensation directly correlates with the exact number of hours clocked. It is essential for determining statutory overtime pay (OT), freelance client billing, and part-time shift wages across Malaysia.

Who Uses Hourly Pay?

Hourly pay rates are widely utilized across multiple employment sectors in Malaysia:

  • Part-Time Employees: Paid strictly based on logged shift hours in retail, F&B, and service industries.
  • Freelancers & Independent Contractors: Utilize hourly rates to quote, invoice, and bill clients for custom project work.
  • Interns & Trainees: May receive hourly or daily stipends instead of a fixed monthly allowance.
  • Contract & Shift Workers: Prevalent in manufacturing, logistics, and healthcare operations.
  • Employers & HR Managers: Rely on hourly rates to calculate statutory overtime pay under the Employment Act 1955.

How to Calculate Hourly Rate

The standard statutory calculation in Malaysia (under the Employment Act 1955) enforces a fixed 26-day monthly divisor, regardless of calendar month length:

Statutory Formula (EA 1955):
Hourly Rate (HRP) = (Monthly Basic Salary ÷ 26) ÷ Normal Daily Work Hours
Pro Tip: For standard 5-day workweeks, actual average monthly working days equal 21.67 days (5 days × 52 weeks ÷ 12 months). Our calculator allows selecting your exact work schedule for personal budgeting.

Factors That Affect Hourly Rate

Several core parameters determine your actual hourly earning power:

  • Weekly Working Schedule: A 5-day workweek yields a higher hourly rate than a 6-day workweek for the same monthly salary.
  • Shift Hours: Working 7 hours daily yields a higher hourly value than an 8-hour shift at identical monthly pay.
  • Fixed Monthly Allowances: Non-variable position or housing allowances are included when computing the Ordinary Rate of Pay (ORP).

Average Hourly Rates in Malaysia

Market benchmarks across various industries and employment types in Malaysia:

  • Retail & F&B Service Staff: RM 7.00 – RM 12.00 / hour
  • Event Promoters & Brand Ambassadors: RM 10.00 – RM 25.00 / hour
  • Freelance Writers, Designers & Developers: RM 30.00 – RM 150.00+ / hour
  • Private Home Tutors & Trainers: RM 40.00 – RM 100.00+ / hour

Minimum Wage & Hourly Pay in Malaysia

Under national statutory Minimum Wage Orders, the mandatory minimum base rate is RM 1,700 / month. Converted hourly rates equal:

  • Statutory Daily Rate (ORP): RM 65.38 / day (RM 1,700 ÷ 26 days)
  • Statutory Minimum Hourly Rate: RM 8.17 / hour (based on 8-hour daily shift)
  • Monthly Minimum Salary Base: RM 1,700 / month

Tips for Part-Time Workers, Freelancers & Interns

When negotiating freelance rates or part-time contract wages in Malaysia, factor in critical self-funded overheads:

  • Statutory Deductions: Account for voluntary self-employed KWSP (i-Saraan) and PERKESO (SKSPS) contributions.
  • Medical & Equipment Costs: Factor in personal health insurance, software licenses, laptop equipment, and travel expenses.
  • Freelance Rate Premium: Set your freelance hourly quote at least 25% – 35% higher than equivalent full-time hourly pay to compensate for unpaid time off and client acquisition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions about calculating your hourly rate in Malaysia? Find answers to the most common questions below.

What is an hourly rate?

An hourly rate is the specific amount of compensation earned for each hour of work performed. It is used to calculate:

  • Part-Time Wages: Hourly compensation for retail, F&B, and contract staff.
  • Freelance Client Invoicing: Billable project rates for contractors and freelancers.
  • Statutory Overtime Pay (OT): Base Hourly Rate of Pay (HRP) under the Employment Act 1955.
How do I calculate hourly pay?

The calculation depends on whether you follow statutory labor law or personal schedule methods:

  • Statutory EA 1955 Method: Enforces a fixed 26-day monthly divisor.
    Hourly Rate (HRP) = (Monthly Basic Salary ÷ 26) ÷ Daily Working Hours
  • Calendar Schedule Method: Calculates schedule-based actual monthly working hours.
    Hourly Rate = Monthly Basic Salary ÷ Total Monthly Working Hours
How many working days are there in a month?

Average working days per month vary based on weekly schedule:

  • 5-Day Workweek: Approximately 21.67 working days per month (5 days × 52 weeks ÷ 12 months).
  • 5.5-Day Workweek: Approximately 23.83 working days per month (5.5 days × 52 weeks ÷ 12 months).
  • 6-Day Workweek: Approximately 26.00 working days per month (6 days × 52 weeks ÷ 12 months).
  • Statutory Employment Act (EA 1955): Strictly fixed at 26 days for daily and hourly rates.
Does hourly pay include overtime?

No. Standard hourly rates cover regular working hours only. Overtime hours are compensated using statutory multipliers under the Employment Act 1955:

  • Normal Workday OT: Paid at 1.5x your Hourly Rate of Pay (HRP).
  • Rest Day OT: Paid at 2.0x your HRP for excess hours.
  • Public Holiday OT: Paid at 3.0x your HRP for excess hours.
What is Malaysia's minimum hourly wage?

Under Malaysia's statutory Minimum Wage Order, the minimum monthly base is RM 1,700. Key statutory minimum benchmarks include:

  • Statutory Minimum Hourly Rate: RM 8.17 / hour (based on 8-hour daily shift).
  • Statutory Daily Rate (ORP): RM 65.38 / day (RM 1,700 ÷ 26 days).
  • Monthly Minimum Salary Base: RM 1,700 / month.
