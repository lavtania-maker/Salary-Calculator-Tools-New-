What is EPF (KWSP)?

The Employees Provident Fund (EPF), or KWSP, is a mandatory retirement savings scheme in Malaysia designed to ensure long-term financial security for employees. Both workers and employers contribute a portion of the monthly salary into the individual's account.

Key uses of EPF savings:

  • • Retirement planning and post-career income
  • • Housing withdrawals for home purchases or loan repayments
  • • Education expenses for yourself or your children
  • • Medical support and emergency financial needs

How EPF Works in Malaysia

Contributions are automatically deducted from your monthly gross salary. The rates are statutory but vary based on age, monthly income level, and citizenship status.

Employee Contribution

A standard percentage (usually 11% for those below 60) is deducted directly from your monthly payslip.

Employer Contribution

Employers provide an additional contribution (12% to 13%) on top of your base salary, deposited into your account.

How to Calculate EPF Contribution

Calculation is simple: multiply your gross monthly salary by the applicable employee and employer rates. For most Malaysians earning below RM5,000, the employer rate is 13%.

Standard Monthly Examples:

  • RM3,000 Salary: RM330 (11%) + RM390 (13%)
  • RM5,000 Salary: RM550 (11%) + RM650 (13%)
  • RM10,000 Salary: RM1,100 (11%) + RM1,200 (12%)

Legal & Compliance

EPF contributions are legally mandatory for all eligible employees in Malaysia. Failure to comply can result in significant legal consequences for employers.

Is it compulsory?

Yes. Employers who fail to contribute by the 15th of each month face heavy fines, penalties, and potential legal action.

Who needs to pay?

  • • All full-time and part-time employees are covered.
  • • Foreign workers and non-citizens can also contribute voluntarily.

Not Included:

  • Service charges
  • Gratuity and retirement benefits

  • Gifts

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I withdraw my EPF savings before retirement?

Yes. Members can make partial or full withdrawals prior to retirement under specified EPF schemes:

  • Account 3 (Akaun Fleksibel): Instant, flexible withdrawals anytime for any purpose with no conditions.
  • Account 2 (Akaun Sejahtera): Approved pre-retirement withdrawals for housing, education, medical expenses, Hajj, or age 50.
  • Full Withdrawal: Permitted upon reaching age 55, incapacitation, or permanent departure from Malaysia.
What happens to my EPF if I resign or change jobs?

Your accumulated EPF savings remain completely secure and intact:

  • Account Continuity: Your EPF member number remains unchanged throughout your career across all private-sector employers.
  • Seamless Onboarding: Simply provide your existing EPF member number to your new employer upon joining.
  • Dividend Accumulation: Existing account balances continue earning annual compounded EPF dividends uninterrupted.
Is EPF contribution mandatory for all employees?

EPF contributions are governed by statutory guidelines depending on residency status:

  • Malaysian Citizens & PRs: Mandatory at standard statutory rates (11% employee / 12-13% employer).
  • Non-Citizens & Foreign Workers: Mandatory starting at a minimum rate of 2% employee + 2% employer contributions.
  • Self-Employed & Gig Workers: Voluntary contributions available via i-Saraan with government matching incentives.
Can I contribute more than the standard EPF rate?

Yes! Both employees and employers can voluntarily elect to contribute above standard rates:

  • Voluntary Employee Rate: Submit Form KWSP 17A (AHL) to increase your monthly payroll deduction rate.
  • Voluntary Employer Rate: Employers can choose to contribute higher employer rates as part of competitive remuneration packages.
  • Self-Deposit (i-Akaun): Make voluntary personal contributions anytime up to RM 100,000 annually.
  • Tax Savings: Maximizes eligible individual income tax relief under EPF and life insurance categories.
What is the difference between EPF Account 1, Account 2, and Account 3?

Monthly contributions are split across three dedicated accounts under EPF's restructured 3-Account system:

  • Account 1 (Akaun Persaraan - 75%): Designated for retirement wealth accumulation until reaching age 55.
  • Account 2 (Akaun Sejahtera - 15%): Allocated for pre-retirement life requirements including housing, healthcare, education, and age 50 withdrawals.
  • Account 3 (Akaun Fleksibel - 10%): Accessible anytime for emergency or immediate short-term financial needs with zero conditions.
