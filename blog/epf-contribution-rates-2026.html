EPF (KWSP) Contribution Rates 2026

By SalaryCalculator.my Team

What is EPF (KWSP)?

EPF, or Kumpulan Wang Simpanan Pekerja (KWSP), is Malaysia's mandatory retirement savings scheme. Both employers and employees must contribute a portion of the employee's monthly wages to EPF every month. Under the EPF Act 1991, employers must deduct and submit contributions by the 15th of the following month. Failure to do so can result in fines, imprisonment, and legal action.

Since 1 October 2025, EPF contributions have also become mandatory for foreign workers in Malaysia — a significant update that all employers must comply with.

What Counts as Wages for EPF?

EPF contributions are calculated based on an employee's wages, which includes:

  • Basic salary
  • Bonuses
  • Allowances
  • Commissions
  • Incentives and overtime pay

Non-wage payments such as travelling allowances, retirement gratuity, and retrenchment benefits are generally not included in EPF calculations.

EPF Account Structure

Your EPF savings are divided into three accounts:

  • Akaun Persaraan (75%) — For retirement only, accessible at age 55
  • Akaun Sejahtera (15%) — For housing, education, and medical needs
  • Akaun Fleksibel (10%) — Can be withdrawn anytime

EPF Dividend

EPF historically declares an annual dividend of 5–6% on your savings. Dividends are credited to your account every year, typically in February or March. Check your balance via i-Akaun at kwsp.gov.my.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the EPF contribution rate for employees in 2026?

Employees below 60 years old contribute 11% of their monthly wages. Employees aged 60 and above contribute 5.5%.

What is the employer EPF contribution rate?

Employers contribute 13% for employees earning RM5,000 and below, and 12% for those earning above RM5,000.

Do foreign workers need to contribute to EPF?

Yes. Since 1 October 2025, EPF contributions are mandatory for foreign workers. Both employee and employer each contribute 2%.

Can an employee opt out of EPF?

Malaysian employees above 55 years old may choose to opt out of EPF contributions.

What if my employer does not contribute to EPF?

You can report your employer to KWSP (EPF) directly. The employer is legally obligated to contribute and may face penalties including fines and imprisonment.

Is EPF contribution tax deductible?

Yes. Employee EPF contributions are tax deductible up to RM4,000 per year under the Malaysian Income Tax Act.
