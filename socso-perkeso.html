What is SOCSO (PERKESO)?

SOCSO (Social Security Organization), known as PERKESO, provides essential social security protection to Malaysian employees through comprehensive coverage schemes.

🏥 Work-related injuries
♿ Disability protection
🩺 Medical support
💰 Loss of income coverage

How SOCSO Contribution Works

SOCSO contribution is calculated based on two main variables: your monthly gross salary and your age.

Gross Monthly Salary

SOCSO is structured by contribution bands:

Age Factor

Your age determines protection scope:

  • ✓ 60 & Above: Employment injury only.

The 10 Types of SOCSO Claims in Malaysia

1. Temporary Disablement Benefit (Faedah Hilang Upaya Sementara)

When: Employee temporarily unable to work due to work-related injury or illness
Benefit: Daily cash allowance until recovery
Example: Arm injury during machine operation requiring rest

2. Permanent Disablement Benefit (Faedah Hilang Upaya Kekal)

When: Permanent loss of function due to workplace accident
Benefit: Monthly pension or lump sum based on disability level
Example: Permanent hearing loss from loud machinery

3. Dependants’ Benefit (Faedah Orang Tanggungan)

When: Employee dies due to work-related accident or disease
Benefit: Monthly pension for dependants
Example: Family supported after workplace accident death

4. Funeral Benefit (Faedah Pengurusan Mayat)

When: Death due to work-related causes
Benefit: One-off payment for funeral costs
Example: Financial aid for funeral arrangements

5. Education Benefit (Faedah Pendidikan)

When: Children of eligible employees receive support
Benefit: Covers education fees and expenses
Example: University tuition support for employee’s child

6. Medical Benefit (Faedah Perubatan)

When: Treatment for work-related injuries or diseases
Benefit: Covers hospital, surgery, and medication
Example: Surgery for work-related injury

7. Rehabilitation Benefit (Faedah Pemulihan)

When: Employee needs recovery support after injury
Benefit: Rehab services, training, or prosthetics
Example: Physiotherapy or prosthetic support

8. Constant Attendance Allowance (Elaun Layan Sentiasa)

When: Employee requires constant care due to disability
Benefit: Monthly allowance for caregiver support
Example: Paralysis requiring full-time care

9. Invalidity Pension (Pencen Ilat)

When: Unable to work due to non-work-related illness
Benefit: Monthly pension
Example: Severe illness preventing continued work

10. Invalidity Grant (Geran Ilat)

When: Does not qualify for invalidity pension
Benefit: One-off lump sum
Example: Early financial support due to illness

What is PERKESO LINDUNG 24 Jam?

PERKESO LINDUNG 24 Jam (Skim Kemalangan Bukan Bencana Kerja) is a social protection scheme introduced by PERKESO to provide financial protection for employees who suffer non-work-related accidents.

It may cover eligible accidents occurring outside working hours, such as:

  • Accidents at home
  • Personal travel within Malaysia
  • Sports and recreational activities
  • Other eligible non-work-related accidents

Eligible benefits may include:

  • Medical Benefits
  • Temporary Disablement Benefit
  • Permanent Disablement Benefit
  • Dependants' Benefit
  • Funeral Benefit
  • Rehabilitation and Return to Work (RTW)

Coverage is subject to PERKESO's terms and eligibility requirements.

PERKESO LINDUNG 24 Jam Contributions

Contributions are currently fully borne by employees, while employers are responsible for deducting and remitting the contributions together with monthly SOCSO contributions.

Period Employee Contribution
1 June 2026 – 31 May 2028 0.75% of monthly wages
1 June 2028 – 31 May 2031 1.00% of monthly wages
From 1 June 2031 1.25% of monthly wages

Is LINDUNG 24 Jam Mandatory?

  • Malaysian employees: Participation is voluntary.
  • Foreign employees: Participation remains mandatory under current PERKESO requirements.

Does LINDUNG 24 Jam Affect SOCSO Contributions?

LINDUNG 24 Jam does not replace or change existing SOCSO employer contribution rates. It is an additional employee contribution for eligible participants while existing SOCSO schemes continue as normal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions about SOCSO (PERKESO) contributions in Malaysia? Find the answers to the most common questions below.

What happens if my employer doesn't pay SOCSO contributions?

Non-compliance with statutory PERKESO contribution regulations incurs strict statutory penalties under the Employees' Social Security Act 1969:

  • Legal Penalties: Non-compliant employers face fines of up to RM10,000 fine, imprisonment of up to 2 years imprisonment, or both.
  • Loss of Coverage: Failure to pay compromises employee eligibility for vital Employment Injury Scheme and Invalidity Scheme protection.
  • Reporting Non-Compliance: Employees can report non-paying employers online via PERKESO's ASSIST Portal or by visiting any physical PERKESO branch nationwide.
Can I withdraw my SOCSO contributions like EPF?

No. Unlike KWSP (EPF), SOCSO functions strictly as a mandatory social insurance scheme rather than an individual savings account:

  • No Lump-Sum Withdrawals: Contributions pooled into national protection funds cannot be withdrawn upon resignation, retirement, or job change.
  • Claim-Based Benefits: Financial payouts and medical benefits are disbursed only upon filing valid claims under the Employment Injury Scheme or Invalidity Scheme.
  • Comprehensive Protection: Provides lifelong medical treatment, temporary/permanent disablement allowances, pensions, and dependants' benefits following workplace accidents or severe illnesses.
Does SOCSO coverage continue after I resign?

No. Standard employment-based coverage ends once active contributions cease, subject to specific claim conditions:

  • Active Employment Term: Standard protection under the Employment Injury Scheme ends on your last day of employment.
  • Historical Claims: You remain eligible to file claims for injuries, occupational diseases, or disabilities incurred during your period of active coverage.
  • New Job Continuation: Your new employer automatically resumes contributions under your existing PERKESO registration.
Are self-employed and gig workers eligible for SOCSO?

Yes! Self-employed workers, e-hailing drivers, delivery riders, and freelancers are covered under dedicated statutory protection:

  • Self-Employment Social Security Scheme (SKSPS): Enables self-employed individuals and gig workers to register and contribute voluntarily.
  • Occupational Protection: Provides medical, temporary disablement, permanent disablement, and dependants' benefits tailored for independent work environments.
  • Online Registration: Convenient enrollment and contribution payments can be processed directly via the PERKESO Matrix Portal or ASSIST Portal.
Does SOCSO cover foreign workers in Malaysia?

Yes. Protection under PERKESO is mandatory for all documented non-citizens and foreign workers:

  • Mandatory Registration: Employers are legally required to register and contribute for all foreign employees under the Employees' Social Security Act 1969.
  • Employment Injury Scheme: Covers foreign workers against workplace accidents, commuting accidents, and occupational diseases.
  • Contribution Rates: Employers contribute 1.25% of the foreign employee's monthly wage, while foreign employees are exempt from employee-side deductions.
